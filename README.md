# Victorian Election 2026

This is my independent small side project taking an applied mathematics and statistical toolkit to the task of investigating electoral change in Victoria.

Developed by [Sam Murphy](https://github.com/itssammurphy) an undergraduate student in mathematics at the University of Melbourne, exploring how statistical inference and computational methods can be used to understand voting patterns, and the uncertainty surrounding the 2026 Victorian state election.

## Overview

The 2026 Victorian state election presents an interesting mathematical problem in that we are trying to investigate electoral change when the historical voting patterns may no longer be reliable indicators.

Historical election results provide detailed information about previous voting behaviour, while opinion polls offer incomplete and noisy ideas of current voting intention.

What can be inferred from these datasets? Let's take a look.

I'm going to start by looking just at my local district of Essendon, before extending methods to other electorates and potentially modelling the whole of Victoria.

## Mathematical approach

I want to kind of use this as an opportunity to combine content from several subjects in my undergraduate degree to show the utility of these tools.

- **Linear Algebra:** dimensionality reduction and transition systems
- **Probability for Inference:** estimation, likelihoods, Bayesian inference
- **Stochastic Modelling:** Markov models, latent-state processes and stochastic simulation
- **Numerical Methods:** optimisation, Monte Carlo methods
- **Applied Mathematical Modelling:** sensitivity analysis and validation
- **Algorithms and Computation:** reproducible data processing and preferential vote handling.

## Independence and Limitations Disclaimer

This is an independent educational and research project. It is not affiliated with the VEC, the University of Melbourne, or any political party. Statistical estimates and simulations should not be interpreted as official election information. Any modelling of future electoral outcomes will depend on incomplete information and explicit assumptions. The main purpose here is to investigate mathematical and statistical properties of electoral data, not to advocate for a particular election outcome.
